DNA, Genetic, Racial & Ethnic Heritage Communities.

This page provides a framework within which activists may work to serve the interests of DNA, Genetic, Racial, Blood-line, & Ethnic Heritage Communities. Every person carries a unique genetic heritage & ethnic identity that deserves to be understood, preserved, & respected. This organizing framework acknowledges the rich diversity of humanity while affirming the common dignity & equal standing before the law of all people, regardless of race or ethnicity. All communities listed here-under are invited to participate in good-faith dialogue & consensus-building within the Common-Law Republic framework.

Major Genetic, Racial & Ethnic Heritage Communities; Listed:

The following represent the principal genetic, racial, & ethnic heritage communities present within the United States & world-wide. This listing is intended as a starting point; & suggestions for additions are welcomed.

  • Native American & Indigenous Peoples Communities.

    The original inhabitants of North America, comprising hundreds of distinct tribal nations, each with unique languages, cultures, & traditions. The Common-Law Republic organizing model deeply respects the sovereignty claims & cultural heritage of all Native & Indigenous peoples.

  • Anglo-Saxon & Germanic Heritage Communities.

    Communities tracing their lineage to the Germanic-speaking peoples of Northern Europe, including England, Germany, the Netherlands, Scandinavia, & related regions. Much of the Common-Law & Republican legal tradition derives from this cultural heritage.

  • Celtic Heritage Communities.

    Communities tracing their ancestry to the Celtic peoples of Ireland, Scotland, Wales, Brittany, & related regions. A rich tradition of tribal self-governance & customary law exists within Celtic heritage.

  • African & African-American Heritage Communities.

    Communities whose ancestry traces to the African continent, representing extraordinary linguistic, cultural, & genetic diversity. A central community in American history & in the ongoing struggle for equal standing before the law & genuine self-governance.

  • Hispanic & Latin Heritage Communities.

    Communities tracing their heritage to Spain, Portugal, Mexico, Central America, South America, & the Caribbean. A rich blend of European, Indigenous, & African heritage forms the foundation of these diverse communities.

  • Ashkenazi & Sephardic Jewish Heritage Communities.

    Communities tracing their ancestry to the ancient Israelite peoples, with distinct Ashkenazi (Eastern European) & Sephardic (Iberian/Mediterranean) lineages. A long tradition of community self-governance & legal scholarship is an important part of this heritage.

  • East Asian Heritage Communities.

    Communities tracing their ancestry to China, Japan, Korea, Taiwan, & related regions. A rich cultural heritage of family loyalty, community discipline, & scholarly achievement characterizes these communities.

  • South Asian Heritage Communities.

    Communities tracing their ancestry to India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka, & related regions. One of the most diverse groupings of human genetic & cultural heritage on earth.

  • Middle Eastern & North African Heritage Communities.

    Communities tracing their ancestry to the Arab world, Persia (Iran), Turkey, the Levant, & North Africa. Ancient cradles of civilization & rich traditions of law, philosophy, & commerce.

  • Slavic Heritage Communities.

    Communities tracing their ancestry to the Slavic peoples of Eastern Europe, including Russia, Ukraine, Poland, the Czech Republic, Slovakia, the Balkans, & related regions.

  • Pacific Islander & Polynesian Heritage Communities.

    Communities tracing their ancestry to Hawaii, Samoa, Tonga, Fiji, the Marshall Islands, & the broader Pacific Island region. Strong traditions of community governance & oral cultural transmission characterize these peoples.

  • Multi-Racial & Mixed-Heritage Communities.

    A growing segment of the American population identifies with multiple racial & ethnic heritages. These communities represent the living reality of human genetic mixing throughout history & are fully welcomed within the Common-Law Republic organizing framework.

Principles for Ethnic & Heritage Community Organizing:

All genetic, racial, & ethnic communities participating in this framework are encouraged to observe the following principles:

  • Affirm the equal standing before the Common Law of all persons, regardless of race or ethnicity.
  • Celebrate & preserve the unique cultural heritage, language, & traditions of each community.
  • Engage in good-faith dialogue & consensus-building across racial & ethnic lines.
  • Promote equal access to justice & equal protection of natural rights for all persons.
  • Support community self-determination & local sovereignty for all heritage communities.
  • Resist agitation, division, & conflict deliberately fomented between communities.
